# Refuge floor: six platform texts (D53)

## Claim ledger
- Claim: the empty, open floor in tall Mumbai towers is a refuge area the rules require, not an unfinished floor.
- Proof: Maharashtra rules (as summarised by Square Yards, Nov 2024): above 24 m, a refuge area on every 7th habitable floor; free of FSI up to 4% of the habitable floor area it serves; it cannot be sold, locked or converted into a flat, kitchen or office (Piramal Realty, Jul 2025); NBC 2016 requires refuge areas above 24 m (Housing.com).
- Caveat: rules differ by state and building height; the approved plan of each building decides. Education only, not advice.

## Facebook
If you or your family live in a tall building, there is one floor worth knowing about.
In Maharashtra, towers taller than 24 metres must keep an open "refuge area" on every seventh floor that people live on. In a fire, the staircase can fill with smoke faster than families can walk down, so people go down only a few floors, step out into open air and wait there for the fire brigade.
That floor can never be sold, locked or made into a flat.
Do you know which floor is the refuge floor in your building?

## LinkedIn
Title: Refuge floors in Mumbai high-rises: what the empty floor is for
For a buyer or investor in a high-rise, the open floor in the middle of the tower is part of the building's approved fire design, not lost space.
In Maharashtra, buildings taller than 24 metres must provide a refuge area on every seventh habitable floor. Up to 4% of the habitable floor area it serves is exempt from FSI, so it does not eat into the developer's buildable area, and it can never be sold, locked or converted.
When a refuge area has been enclosed or turned into a unit, that is a compliance question worth asking about before you sign.
Sources: Square Yards (Nov 2024), Piramal Realty (Jul 2025), Housing.com.
