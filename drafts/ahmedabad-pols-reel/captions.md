## Facebook
If you grew up in an old city neighbourhood, this will feel familiar.

In old Ahmedabad, families lived in "pols": a lane of 50 to 100 homes that share walls and sit behind one big gate. In the 1700s, when the Mughals and the Marathas fought over the city and robbers got past the old city walls, neighbours closed their pols like small forts. A guard lived in a room above the gate, the rooftops were joined from house to house, and some narrow passages were made to look like the front of a home so that families could slip out quietly in an attack. In 2017 this old city became the first Indian city on the UNESCO World Heritage list.

Title: Ahmedabad's pols: the original gated community and what it teaches about security design

Gated living in India is not new. Ahmedabad's pols, lanes of 50 to 100 houses sharing walls behind one gate, became fortress-like in the 1700s when city walls stopped keeping robbers out (Deccan Herald; Wikipedia).
Their design choices are familiar: one controlled entry with a guard room above it, shared walls, joined terraces and concealed exits disguised as house fronts (Live History India).
The trade-off then, as now: security and community in exchange for density, narrow access and harder redevelopment. The walled city became India's first UNESCO World Heritage City in 2017.
