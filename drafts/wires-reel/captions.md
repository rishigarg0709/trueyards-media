# Why India looks like this #7: the wires on every pole (28 Nov 2026 evening)

## Claim ledger
- Claim: much of the black tangle on Indian street poles is not electricity but cable TV, internet and optical fibre lines hung on power poles, often without permission.
- Proof: BESCOM told operators to remove unauthorised optical fibre, data and dish cables from its poles by 8 Jul 2024 (PTI via Siasat, 6 Jul 2024); a BESCOM survey found about 2,335.82 km of illegal wires in Bengaluru (Deccan Herald, 19 Jul 2024); at SG Palya cables were back the next day (Deccan Herald, Jul 2024).
- Caveat: no one has counted what share of wires India-wide are data lines; power lines usually, not always, run at the top; the 2,335 km is one city's survey; education only.

## Facebook
Look at the electricity pole outside your house. Most of the black wires wrapped around it are probably not carrying power at all.
They are cable TV, internet and fibre lines, tied onto electricity poles by the companies that sell those connections. In July 2024 the Bengaluru power company, BESCOM, gave operators a deadline of 8 July to take their unauthorised cables down or see them removed. Its own survey had found about 2,335 km of illegal wires in that one city. On at least one street the cables were back the very next day.

Title: Why Indian street poles are buried in data cables
Every new broadband connection adds a physical line, and in most Indian cities that line is hung on the nearest electricity pole.
In July 2024 BESCOM ordered operators to remove unauthorised fibre, data and dish cables from its poles; its survey had found about 2,335 km of illegal wiring in Bengaluru. Removal was partial, and on some streets cables returned within a day.
For anyone assessing a locality, overhead clutter is a signal of how utilities are planned and maintained, and of who carries the safety risk.
Sources: BESCOM via PTI, Deccan Herald, The News Minute.
