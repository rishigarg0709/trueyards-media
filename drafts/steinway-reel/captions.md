## Facebook
Imagine a building as tall as six Qutub Minars, but narrower than a cricket pitch is long.

That is Steinway Tower in New York: about 435 metres high and only about 18 metres wide. A tower that thin moves in the wind, and in a strong storm its top can sway close to a metre. To keep it calm, engineers put a huge weight of steel plates near the top that swings the opposite way, and anchored the base deep into the rock below. Each of its 60 homes sold for well over a hundred crore rupees.

Would you be comfortable living on the top floor of a building that sways?

For developers on small, costly plots, slenderness is a trade-off between land and engineering.
Steinway Tower in New York rises about 435 m on a footprint about 18 m wide (HowStuffWorks). That needs a tuned mass damper above the top floor, two concrete shear walls and about 200 rock anchors into bedrock (Architectural Record, 2021; KSL, 2022).
The payoff: just 60 homes, each sold in 2022 for roughly ₹140 crore to ₹520 crore at the 2022 exchange rate.
