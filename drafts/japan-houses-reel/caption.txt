Why Japanese houses lose value like cars

In Japan, a 30-year-old house can be worth almost nothing. The land keeps its value. The building does not.

Japan's tax rules give a wooden house a useful life of 22 years. Only about 1 in 7 homes sold is second-hand. After the 1981 earthquake code, older homes feel risky, so many are torn down and rebuilt. Average age at demolition: about 32 years in Japan, about 66 in the US, about 80 in the UK.
