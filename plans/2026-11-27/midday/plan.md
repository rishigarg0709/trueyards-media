## Verified facts
1. Mumbai Santacruz average annual rainfall 2,502.3 mm (IMD normals 1991 to 2020, Wikipedia Climate of Mumbai table). June 526.3, July 919.9, August 560.8, September 383.5 mm: 2,390.5 mm, about 95% of the year in four months (our arithmetic). VO "lagbhag dhai metre" and "zyada hissa sirf chaar mahine mein". (Free Press Journal, Sep 2024, quotes a lower average of 2,319 mm; both round to "over two metres", so VO says "lagbhag dhai metre" with the IMD table as source.)
2. Algae and cyanobacteria create a biofouling film on building surfaces, in colours from green to grey and black; a facade often wet by rain promotes it; porous materials such as brick, stone and mortar are at risk (RIBuild, Algae growth).
3. Dark stains mostly match water run-off marks below or near roof parapets and window sills (Viegas et al., Lisbon facades study, LNEC repository). Berger Paints names areas under projections where water drips and dries slowly, and damp terraces near parapets.
4. Black or dark fungi such as Cladosporium are found in such stains (Viegas et al.); black mould on Indian walls is usually Aspergillus or Cladosporium (AapkaPainter). VO says only "algae, cyanobacteria aur fungus".
5. In many Indian cities monsoon moisture, warm air and shade create ideal conditions (Berger Paints); humid cities like Mumbai, Kolkata, Kochi and Chennai are named (AapkaPainter).
6. Fix order: remove growth before painting, seal cracks, waterproof parapets and chajjas, then anti-algal and anti-fungal paint (Berger Paints). No brand named in the reel.
Dropped: any claim that pollution causes none of it (soot can add to it; VO says "Pollution? Nahi." as the hook and the payoff shows the main cause is biological; caption says "mostly"). Dropped genus Gloeocapsa (not in the sources read).

## Caption draft (Instagram)
Why are Mumbai's old buildings black at the top? 🏢
It is mostly not soot. It is alive.
Mumbai gets about 2.5 metres of rain a year, about 95% of it from June to September. Rain runs off the parapet and the window ledges, the plaster stays wet, and algae, cyanobacteria and fungi grow into a dark film. That is why the blackest lines sit right under the roof and the ledges.
So a plain repaint rarely lasts: clean the growth, seal the cracks, waterproof the parapet, then use an anti-algal coat.
Send this to your society secretary before the next repaint meeting.
