## Verified facts
1. Census 2011: 11,89,144 vacant houses in Kerala (5,85,998 rural, 6,03,146 urban); 1,96,058 of them had four rooms, 73,975 five rooms and 57,272 more than six rooms (KSSP report citing Census 2011, The News Minute). So over 3 lakh vacant houses had four or more rooms (our sum, 3,27,305).
2. About 11% of Kerala's roughly 1 crore houses were vacant, vs 7.45% nationally (ThePrint, 19 Mar 2023, citing 2011 Census data). Say "about one in ten".
3. Kerala Migration Survey 2023: about 22 lakh emigrants (21 lakh in 2018); remittances ₹2,16,893 crore in 2023; about 80% of emigrants in GCC countries (19.5% beyond the GCC) (KMS 2023 findings, IIMAD; summary via KSG India).
4. Kerala's home building is "flush with money earned abroad"; a house on a piece of land keeps migrants rooted to their hometowns (Gulf Times). VO says "much of Kerala's home building runs on money earned abroad", not a measured share.
5. Pathanamthitta: over 61,000 closed homes, Local Self Government Department figures obtained by South First (29 Oct 2025). The last census showed a population decline in Pathanamthitta and Idukki (same article).
6. The 3 Feb 2023 Kerala budget proposed a tax on vacant houses; it was withdrawn within a month (ThePrint, 19 Mar 2023).
Dropped: any claim that a specific share of remittances goes to houses; any current statewide vacant count (no Census since 2011).

Why Kerala has lakhs of locked, empty houses
