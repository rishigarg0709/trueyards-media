## Verified facts
1. Civil Lines were residential neighbourhoods developed in the British Raj for senior civilian officers such as the Divisional Commissioner and District Magistrate, historically called "White Town", distinct from forts and cantonments, which were military establishments, and from the "native" areas (Wikipedia, Civil Lines).
2. In Lucknow, the Civil Lines was the residential area for the European non-military community, adjacent to the cantonment and insulated from the old city; its basic unit was the "bungalow compound"; the cantonment was a separate administrative unit outside municipal limits (NPTEL, Urban Planning lecture 38).
3. Britannica: residential areas originally built by the British for senior officers; marked by openness, green grass, trees and older one-storey bungalows, in contrast with the street network of Old Delhi.
4. Prayagraj (Allahabad) Civil Lines: laid out in 1858, a few months after the end of the 1857 rebellion (the infobox says 1857; we use 1858 from the text and say "right after 1857"), eight villages taken to form it, described as the largest town-planning project in India before New Delhi; today an upper-class business district with high-rises (Wikipedia, Civil Lines, Prayagraj).
5. Delhi Civil Lines: the hub for British officials until New Delhi was established (1911); Maidens Hotel built there in 1903; the Viceroy vacated 1 Alipore Road in 1930; the Old Secretariat offices moved in 1931; today Raj Niwas (Lieutenant Governor) and the Chief Minister's official residence are there (Wikipedia, Civil Lines, Delhi).
6. Indian cities with a Civil Lines article: Prayagraj, Bareilly, Budaun, Delhi, Jabalpur, Jaipur, Jhansi, Kanpur, Moradabad, Nagpur, Roorkee, Raipur (Wikipedia list). Slides name six of them.

## Caption draft (Instagram)
Why do so many Indian cities have a Civil Lines? 🏛️
Because the British built many Indian cities as three towns: the old city, the cantonment for soldiers, and Civil Lines for the officers who ran the district, set apart from both, with big bungalow compounds on wide, tree-lined roads.
In Allahabad (Prayagraj), the Civil Lines was laid out in 1858, months after the 1857 revolt, and was India's biggest town-planning job before New Delhi. In Delhi, Civil Lines was the officials' quarter until New Delhi was built; the Viceroy moved out in 1930.
