## Verified facts
1. BESCOM (Bengaluru Electricity Supply Company) told service providers to remove unauthorised optical fibre, data and dish cables from its electrical poles by 8 July 2024, said it would remove them itself after that, hold operators responsible for incidents and take legal action (PTI via Siasat, 6 Jul 2024).
2. A BESCOM survey found about 2,335.82 km of illegal wires hanging around the city (Deccan Herald, 19 Jul 2024). VO: "about 2,335 kilometres".
3. After the deadline few companies removed cables and removal happened only in a few places; at SG Palya cables were back the very next day, looped through buildings instead of BESCOM poles (Deccan Herald, after 8 Jul 2024).
4. BBMP said it had stopped issuing permits for new cables and was removing illegal ones (same Deccan Herald report).
5. In 2019 BBMP removed optical fibre cables in Bellandur, Sarjapur Main Road and other Mahadevapura areas; the operators' body COAI said operators had permission and the cables went overhead after civic works removed underground ones (The News Minute, 3 May 2019). Used only as context in the caption, not in VO.
6. A BESCOM statement called the cables a threat to citizens and to its infrastructure; a pole collapse injured a 21-year-old student in SG Palya (Deccan Herald). VO does not describe the injury; on screen only "a safety risk".
Dropped: any India-wide km figure, per-pole rent figures, and "most wires" (not counted anywhere); VO says "a lot of these wires". Dropped "power lines are always on top" as a rule; VO says "usually".
