## Sources (RERA Act 2016, India Code text)
- Section 14(3): a structural defect or defect in workmanship, quality or services brought to the promoter's notice within five years from the date of handing over possession must be rectified without further charge within 30 days, else the allottee is entitled to compensation. https://www.indiacode.nic.in/bitstream/123456789/19012/1/reraact.pdf
- Section 11(4)(b): the promoter must obtain the completion or occupancy certificate and make it available to the allottees. (same)
- Section 19(10): the allottee must take physical possession within two months of the occupancy certificate. (same)
- Section 2(k): carpet area is the net usable floor area, excluding external walls, service shafts, exclusive balcony or verandah and exclusive open terrace, including internal partition walls. (same)
- Section 17(1): the promoter must execute a registered conveyance deed in the allottee's favour, with the undivided proportionate title in common areas to the association; where no local law says otherwise, within three months of the occupancy certificate. (same)
- Housing.com, defects after possession: seepage, leaking bathroom pipes, wiring faults, cracked plaster or flooring; inferior fittings where the agreement defined specifications. https://housing.com/news/compensation-for-defects-in-construction-after-possession-under-rera
- Housing.com, builder warranty under RERA: hairline plaster cracks and normal wear and tear are usually not covered. https://housing.com/news/what-is-builder-warranty-under-rera
- Housiey, defect liability period: notify the builder in writing; keep photos, video, the possession letter and dates. https://housiey.com/blogs/?p=10840
- Note: one UP-RERA quote (Times of India) counts the 5 years from the OC; the Act says from handing over possession. Slides follow the Act.
- Dropped: any carpet-area tolerance percentage (not verified tonight).
