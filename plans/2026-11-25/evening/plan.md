## Sources
- UNESCO World Heritage Centre, Historic City of Ahmadabad (decision 41COM 8B.17, inscribed 2017): founded by Sultan Ahmad Shah I in 1411; pols are residential streets entered through a gate (khadki); a pol has 50 to 100 closely packed houses sharing side walls; bird feeders (chabutaro), community well, pol panchayat. https://whc.unesco.org/en/urban-heritage-atlas/ahmedabad
- Live History India, "Ahmedabad: Live Life Pol-Size" (29 Jan 2020): gateway with a residence above it for a guard; heavy wooden gates; "Many of them were interconnected through narrow passages, some disguised as the facades of homes", one near Shantinath ni Pol; houses interconnected to move roof to roof; tanka water tanks up to 40 feet deep; first Indian city to become a UNESCO World Heritage City in 2017; the British removed pol gates after 1857. https://livehistoryindia.com/story/places/ahmedabad-live-life-pol-size
- Deccan Herald, "Perennial pols of Ahmedabad" (25 Sep 2022): communal clashes in the Maratha and Mughal era made fortress-like settlements with a gate necessary; "Secret doorways provided a safe passage for escape in emergencies"; interconnected terraces. https://www.deccanherald.com/amp/story/features%2Fperennial-pols-of-ahmedabad-1147349.html
- Wikipedia, Historic City of Ahmadabad: pol housing took shape during divided rule 1738 to 1753; as city walls decayed, the pol gate became the protection from robbers; 356 pols in 1872. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Old_Ahmedabad
- Not used: a total count of pols today (sources say 300 to 600, they disagree); "5.35 sq km walled city" (not found tonight).

## Caption draft (Instagram)
Ahmedabad's pols: why some old house fronts were really escape routes.

A pol is a gated lane of 50 to 100 houses sharing walls, inside the walled city Sultan Ahmad Shah I founded in 1411. Many took their fortress shape in the 1700s, when Mughals and Marathas fought over the city and its walls no longer kept robbers out. Each pol had a heavy gate with a guard room above it, rooftops joined house to house, and narrow passages, some disguised as the fronts of homes, for escape in an attack. In 2017 the old city became a UNESCO World Heritage City.
