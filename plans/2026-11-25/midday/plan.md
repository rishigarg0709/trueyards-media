## Sources
- Housing.com, "Undivided share of land (UDS)": definition, formula (flat's super built-up area / total super built-up area of all flats x land area), worked example of 200 flats on 40,000 sq ft (100 x 500, 50 x 1,000, 50 x 1,500 sq ft = 1,75,000 sq ft), giving about 228 sq ft for a 1,000 sq ft flat; UDS should be in the sale deed; on redevelopment the area you get depends on UDS; built structure depreciates, price rise is mostly land. https://housing.com/news/undivided-share-uds/
- Brigade Group blog, "What is undivided share of land": all apartment owners collectively own the entire land, each an undivided share; sale deed should state UDS. https://brigadegroup.com/blog/residential/what-is-undivided-share-of-land
- BankBazaar, "All about undivided share of land": compensation depends on UDS; land prices keep escalating while the constructed area depreciates. https://blog.bankbazaar.com/all-about-undivided-share-of-land
- Arithmetic check (ours): 1,000 / 1,75,000 x 40,000 = 228.6 sq ft.

Flat or plot? Every Indian family has this argument at least once.

A flat comes with an undivided share of land (UDS): your proportionate slice of the plot the building stands on. In one worked example, 200 flats share 40,000 sq ft, so a 1,000 sq ft flat gets about 228 sq ft of land. A plot owner holds the whole piece. A flat owner gets security, lifts and a ready home.

Before you sign, check that your sale deed states your UDS. If the building is ever redeveloped, that number matters.
