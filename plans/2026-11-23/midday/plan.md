## Verified facts
1. Kerala Kaumudi, 9 July 2025 (Big cabal behind 'Plot for Sale' scam in Thiruvananthapuram's Jawahar Nagar): a house and property in Jawahar Nagar were "sold for Rs. 1.5 crore without the information of the real owner, who is now in the United States"; a 76-year-old woman impersonated the owner and signed at the Sasthamangalam Sub-Registrar's Office; a 27-year-old woman posed as the owner's foster daughter; both were arrested; the caretaker found "someone else had paid the tax" when he went to pay it; "The fraudsters mostly select houses that have been uninhabited for years."
2. Housing.com (Illegal sale of property in India): "Fraudsters target properties that have been left unattended for a long time"; remedies include a police complaint and a civil suit; an illegal sale deed can be challenged (cancellation within three years per its FAQ; not used in VO).
3. NRI Legal Services (Land kabza, what to do, 2019): land left unattended is an easy target; owners are advised to keep mutation updated ("not one time process"), keep tax and utility receipts, inspect regularly, fence vacant plots and put up a signboard: "The signboard helps to know that someone owns the land."
4. 1acre.in (Common land scams): the scammer "poses as the owner or a POA holder"; pull the encumbrance record yourself "from the sub-registrar. Never accept the seller's copy"; check mutation history.
Softened: no source states WHY owners paint "not for sale" in those exact words, so the VO says it is a warning sign that the owner is watching (the NRI source's signboard logic), and calls it something you have seen, not a measured trend. No faces, no names of the people in the case on screen or in VO.

Why Indian walls say "This property is NOT for sale"
