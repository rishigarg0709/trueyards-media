## Verified facts
1. Jaipur was founded by Jai Singh II on 18 November 1727, designed by Vidyadhar Bhattacharya, one of India's earliest planned cities, laid out in nine blocks (Wikipedia, Jaipur).
2. The city was painted pink to welcome Albert Edward, Prince of Wales, in 1876; pink was associated with hospitality or welcome (Wikipedia, Jaipur; Mental Floss; Culture Trip).
3. Maharaja Sawai Ram Singh II ordered the painting (Mental Floss; Culture Trip).
4. In 1877 a law is said to have made pink compulsory for buildings in the old city (Mental Floss, Culture Trip). Popular accounts only, so the script says "reportedly". Do not claim the 1877 law is still enforced today.
5. Historian Giles Tillotson suggests the city may have been pinkish before, imitating red sandstone, and the 1876 job was a repaint (Mental Floss). Keep as nuance in the caption, not the hook.
6. 2017: Jaipur Smart City planned about ₹14.6 crore to restore market facades in nine areas using lime plaster and the original "khamira" colour (PTI via Business Standard, 22 Jun 2017).
7. 6 July 2019: UNESCO inscribed the walled city ("Jaipur City, Rajasthan") as a World Heritage Site (Wikipedia, Jaipur; Drishti IAS).
8. Albert Hall (now a museum) was built in the prince's honour (Culture Trip).
Caveat: one 2017 PTI line says the "pink city" name dates from 1853; most sources say 1876. Script uses 1876 only.
Dropped: "no other city has done this" (unverifiable), Hawa Mahal build year (not needed).

## Caption draft
Why is Jaipur pink? 1876, a royal welcome, and a law that followed.

Jaipur was founded on 18 November 1727 as one of India's earliest planned cities. In 1876 Maharaja Sawai Ram Singh II had the old city painted pink, a colour of welcome, for the visiting Prince of Wales. A law in 1877 is said to have made pink compulsory there, and in 2017 the city planned about ₹14.6 crore to restore bazaar facades in the original colour. Some historians think the city was pinkish even before, and 1876 was a repaint.
