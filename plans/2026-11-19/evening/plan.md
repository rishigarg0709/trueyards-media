## Verified facts
1. The Bombay Rents, Hotel and Lodging House Rates Control Act, 1947 set standard rents at the rent of 1 September 1940 (Tandel et al., IDFC Institute and NYU Marron Institute, Decline of Rental Housing in India: the case of Mumbai, 2015; Basis Point Insight).
2. The Maharashtra Rent Control Act, 1999 caps increases at not more than 4% a year from when the standard rent was fixed (Tandel et al.). Bloomberg (2012) mentions 5% under a bill; use "about 4% a year" and keep the caveat.
3. "Standard rent can be one-thousandth, or less, of the prevailing market rent" (Tandel et al.).
4. The island city had 257,186 rent-controlled residential units in the 2010 data (Tandel et al.). Optional on-screen number.
5. About 14,000 cessed buildings in Mumbai, mainly in South Mumbai (Free Press Journal, 24 Feb 2020).
6. Bloomberg, 26 Jun 2012 ("Tossed out 20 tenants turn millionaires in Mumbai"): a couple paid under $20 a month for decades, $23 recently, for a 2,600 sq ft flat on Nepean Sea Road where market rents typically exceed $2,000 a month; the developer paid them $2.5 million and they moved out. Do not name the tenants or the developer.
7. Pagdi: a new tenant pays a premium of about 80% of ownership value, typically split two-thirds to the outgoing tenant and one-third to the landlord; the 1999 Act recognised pagdi (Tandel et al.). Caption only.
8. Urban India's rented share fell from 54% (1961) to 31% (2011) (Tandel et al.). Caption only.

## Caption draft
The South Mumbai flat that rented for about ₹1,300 a month

In 2012 Bloomberg reported a family paying about ₹1,300 a month for a 2,600 sq ft flat on Nepean Sea Road, where market rent was over ₹1 lakh. The reason: Bombay's 1947 rent law froze rents at 1940 levels, and the 1999 Act allows only small yearly increases. Researchers found controlled rent can be one-thousandth of market rent. Landlords stop repairing, about 14,000 old cessed buildings age, and yet tenancies change hands for pagdi worth about 80% of ownership value. When that building was redeveloped, the tenants were paid about ₹14 crore to move. (Rupee figures converted from Bloomberg's dollar figures at the 2012 rate.)
