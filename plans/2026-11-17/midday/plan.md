## Verified facts (all checked 2026-11-16)
1. Statutory useful life for tax depreciation: wooden residential building 22 years, reinforced concrete 47 years (Japan tax rules, as summarised by INA and e-housing.jp).
2. New homes were 85.3% of residential sales in Japan in 2013 (MLIT, via Real Estate Japan), so second-hand homes were about 15%, roughly 1 in 7. Use "about 1 in 7" and label the year in fine print.
3. Average age of demolished homes: Japan 32.1 years, US 66.6, UK 80.6 (MLIT figures cited by e-housing.jp). Say "about 32 / about 66 / about 80".
4. New seismic standard from June 1981; pre-1981 homes face home loan and loan-deduction limits (INA).
5. 9,002,000 vacant homes, 13.8% of all dwellings, both record highs (Statistics Bureau of Japan, 2023 Housing and Land Survey). Use on screen only as a mini-reveal card if time allows; not in VO.

## Voiceover script (final, 110 words)
In Japan... a thirty-year-old house can be worth almost nothing. [short pause] Not the land. The house.

Here's the strange part. Japan's tax rules give a wooden house a useful life of just twenty-two years. After that, on paper... it's done.

And buyers agree. Only about one in seven homes sold is second-hand. Everyone wants new.

Why? [short pause] Earthquakes. The building code got much stricter in 1981, so older homes feel risky. Many get torn down and rebuilt.

So Japanese homes are demolished at about thirty-two years old. In America, about sixty-six. In Britain, about eighty.

Which means in Japan, you're really buying the land. [pause] Would you buy a house that's worth almost nothing in thirty years?
