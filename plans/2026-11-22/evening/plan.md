## Verified facts
1. Kempegowda Bus Station (Majestic) is on the site of Dharmambudhi Lake, which dried up in the early 20th century; construction started in 1965 and it opened in 1969 (Wikipedia, Kempegowda Bus Station); the lake was drained in the early 1900s and the Dharmambudhi ground filled in for the central bus terminus in the early 1960s (Nagendra and Unnikrishnan, Arcadia, Environment and Society Portal, 2019).
2. Other lakes and what stands on them (The News Minute, 28 May 2016, from the Karnataka environment portal list): Sampangi lake, sports stadium; Shoolay lake, football stadium; Akkithimmanahalli lake, hockey stadium; Challaghatta lake, golf course; Koramangala lake, residential layout; Domlur lake, BDA layout.
3. "Bengaluru had close to 261 lakes in 1961" (The News Minute, 2016).
4. Tank area in the Greater Bangalore region: 2,342 ha in 1973 to 918 ha in 2007, a decline of about 60.8% (Ramachandra and Kumar 2008, via Citizen Matters, 11 Jan 2016).
5. Encroachment of lakes and blocked storm-water canals (rajakaluves) are linked to flooding (Environment Support Group quoted in The News Minute; Citizen Matters).
6. Bangalore was founded by Kempe Gowda in the mid-16th century, and the lake was one of its main water sources (Arcadia 2019). We do not say Kempe Gowda built the lake.

Bengaluru had close to 261 lakes in 1961, and between 1973 and 2007 the lake area of Greater Bengaluru fell by about 61%.
