## Verified facts
1. RERA 2016 carpet area: "the net usable floor area of an apartment, excluding the area covered by the external walls, areas under services shafts, exclusive balcony or verandah area and exclusive open terrace area, but includes the area covered by the internal partition walls of the apartment" (as quoted by Housing.com; Act section 2(k)).
2. Super built-up area adds a share of common areas: corridors, staircases, lift areas, lobbies, clubhouses and other common amenities (Business Standard, 11 Jun 2026; ICICI Bank).
3. Loading: "approximately 30%-35%" (Housing.com, quoting a developer); "a loading factor of 1.30 is considered sufficient", super built-up = carpet x (1 + loading) (ICICI Bank). So 1,200 / 1.30 = about 923 sq ft carpet (our arithmetic, labelled "example, 30% loading").
4. UP RERA (Business Standard, 11 Jun 2026): buyers should rely on carpet area rather than super built-up area; carpet area is the legally recognised measure under RERA 2016; a flat marketed at 1,500 sq ft super built-up may have only around 1,000 to 1,100 sq ft carpet.
5. Money example (our arithmetic, labelled example): ₹1 crore / 1,200 = about ₹8,333 per sq ft; ₹1 crore / 923 = about ₹10,834 per sq ft.
Dropped or softened: "builders must sell on carpet area" (disputed in Housing.com; BS says super built-up disclosure is not mandatory); we say only that carpet area is the legal measure under RERA and the number to ask for.
Caveat for fine print: "Example with 30% loading; loading varies by project (about 20 to 35% in the sources)."

## Caption draft
Your 1,200 sq ft flat is not 1,200 sq ft of floor

Brochures usually quote super built-up area: your carpet area plus your walls and a share of the lobby, lifts, stairs and clubhouse. That extra, called loading, is often around 30%. So a "1,200 sq ft" flat can have only about 920 sq ft of carpet. At ₹1 crore, that is about ₹8,300 per sq ft on paper, but about ₹10,800 per sq ft of floor you actually live on. Under RERA 2016, carpet area is the legal measure, so it is the first number to ask for.
