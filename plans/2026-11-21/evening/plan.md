## Verified facts
1. Measured haveli: Bikaner, Rajasthan; about 250 years old; walls 700 mm thick; courtyard 2.9 m x 2.9 m; logged every three hours on 21 June (summer) and 22 December (winter). June: outside 45°C to 25°C; courtyard maximum 43°C, minimum 26°C; living room 32°C to 23°C; bedroom 30°C to 25°C (Verma, Kamal and Brar, "An Appraisal of Vernacular Architecture of Bikaner: Climatic Responsiveness and Thermal Comfort of Havelis", ISVS e-journal 9(2), April 2022).
2. Same study: the haveli walls give a time lag of almost 10 hours (0 to 2 hours for the contemporary house it was compared with); the courtyard is shaded at the second-floor terrace level by a projecting sunshade; the neighbouring havelis shade its walls; the small, deep courtyard is the only ventilation path for the interior, and the temperature difference keeps air flowing in.
3. Same study, winter: outside 20°C to 3°C; bedroom 16°C to 11°C (the thick walls keep heat in). Optional line, not in the script.
4. Context (secondary, not for on-screen numbers): a 2017 review (Pandey, IJCRT 5(1)) describes the aangan as a shaded courtyard and buoyancy ("stack effect") ventilation in courtyard houses; an Old Delhi courtyard house was found to keep a stable indoor temperature thanks to thick brick walls (Yanagisawa et al., AIJ Journal of Technology and Design 23(54), 2017).
Caveats for fine print: one haveli measured over one summer day; the cooling comes from courtyard, shade and thick walls together, not the hole alone. Dropped: "every old house had one", "flats dropped it because of land cost" (no source found tonight), the 3 to 5°C cool-air-pool claim (secondary only).

## Caption draft
Why old Indian houses had a hole in the middle

The aangan, the open courtyard at the heart of old Indian homes, was not just for family life. In a 250-year-old haveli in Bikaner, researchers logged 45°C outside on 21 June. The courtyard reached 43°C, but the bedroom beside it never went above 30°C, with no AC. Three things did it: a small, deep courtyard with a sunshade that keeps the rooms in shade, walls about 70 cm thick that delay the heat by about 10 hours, and air drawn in through the courtyard.

Send this to the family group, to whoever grew up in a house with an aangan.
