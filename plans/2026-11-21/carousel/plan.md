## Verified facts
1. H1 2026 EMI-to-income ratio (Knight Frank India Affordability Index, H1 2026): MMR 69%, NCR 67%, Hyderabad 41%, Bengaluru 35%, Chennai 29%, Pune 28%, Kolkata 25%, Ahmedabad 23% (Business Standard 3 Jul 2026; Angel One 3 Jul 2026; Free Press Journal 3 Jul 2026).
2. Six of eight unchanged vs 2025; Bengaluru 34% to 35% and NCR 66% to 67% worsened marginally (Business Standard 3 Jul 2026).
3. Series (Knight Frank table reproduced by Passionate In Marketing, 4 Jul 2026, revised methodology on residential prices): NCR 2021 43%, 2022 48%, 2023 52%, 2024 66%, 2025 66%, H1 2026 67%. MMR 2016 77%, 2021 65%, H1 2026 69% (FPJ also gives 77% in 2016 and 2019). NCR's rise of 24 points since 2021 is the largest of the 8 (MMR +4, Hyderabad +5, Bengaluru +5, Chennai +1, Pune 0, Kolkata 0, Ahmedabad -2; our arithmetic from the same table).
4. Threshold: "An EMI-to-income ratio above 50% is generally considered unaffordable, as banks are typically reluctant to underwrite home loans beyond that level" (FPJ); "affordable when a household spends 50% or less of its income on home loan EMIs" (Business Standard).
5. Assumptions: 20-year loan, 80% loan to value, average home loan rates, city-average unit size and price (Knight Frank assumptions as listed by Business Standard and PIM).
6. Rate context: RBI cut 125 bps in all and paused at 5.25% in February and June 2026 (Business Standard 3 Jul 2026). Optional for the caption only.
Caveats: city averages, not any one family; the index uses a revised price methodology, so do NOT compare with the older December 2025 edition (which showed Mumbai 47%); the 2021 NCR value comes from the reproduced Knight Frank table, so cite "Knight Frank" with the H1 2026 report. Before building, if Knight Frank has published a newer edition (H2 2026 normally arrives in late December), use the newer one.
