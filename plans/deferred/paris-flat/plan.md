## Verified facts (checked 2026-11-17)
1. Tour Montparnasse: about 210 m, completed 1973 (Paris.fr 50th anniversary page; Euronews gives 209 m and 1973). Use "210 metres".
2. After it was built, Paris capped new buildings at 37 m in 1977 (Euronews 14 Jun 2023: "certain parts of the city"; Planetizen: "37-metre height limit for new buildings in Paris"). VO says "capped new buildings across much of the city"; fine print says "in large parts of Paris".
3. In 2010 Mayor Bertrand Delanoë raised the limit to 180 m for offices and 50 m for housing (Euronews; Planetizen).
4. June 2023: the new bioclimatic local urban plan (PLU) brings back a 12-storey / 37 m cap for new buildings (Euronews; Planetizen; Dezeen).
5. Tour Triangle (Herzog and de Meuron): about 180 m, 42 floors, Porte de Versailles (15th); Paris councillors rejected it on 17 Nov 2014 and approved it on 30 Jun 2015; completion listed as 2026 (Wikipedia; Ulysse: opening planned for the second half of 2026, exact date not announced). Say "due to be finished in 2026", not "opened".
6. La Défense, the business district with most of the region's skyscrapers, sits just west of the Paris city limits (Courbevoie, Puteaux, Nanterre) (Wikipedia La Défense via Wikiwand; Euronews calls it Paris's business district).
7. Montparnasse is widely criticised as out of place, often called the city's most hated building (Euronews "eyesore or icon?"; The B1M "the plan to save Paris' most hated building"). Say "often called the most hated building in Paris".
Dropped: "Paris has only one skyscraper" (false: there are other towers, e.g. in the 13th); the popular joke about the best view being from Montparnasse (unsourced attribution); exact vote date of the 2023 PLU (sources say June 2023, final adoption later).

Parisians hated it. It's often called the most hated building in the city.
